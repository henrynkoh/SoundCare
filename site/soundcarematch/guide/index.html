The 30 care points

BestCareMatch uses 30 care points on a free basic provider profile, and publishes 36 comparison topics. These 30 are topics 1 through 30 of that public comparison: capacity through housekeeping. Payment sits on its own page because a South Sound family has to settle it before a visit.

Who can move in

1. Resident capacity

How many residents is this home licensed for, and how many live here today?

Washington adult family homes are small. A full house changes the care your person will actually get.

2. Provider background

Who owns the home, and do the owners provide the daily care?

In these counties the owner is often the caregiver. Ask who is in the house on a Tuesday night.

3. Dementia support

Is dementia specialty care on the license, and what stage do you support?

A memory need is common after a stay at St. Peter, Capital Medical, Providence Centralia, or Mason General.

4. Mental health support

Do you support mental health needs, and is that specialty on the license?

A website phrase is not the same as a specialty the state has recorded.

5. Developmental disability support

Do you support adults with developmental disabilities, and how do you decide fit?

Fit is individual. Ask what they cannot take as well as what they can.

6. Respite and short stays

Do you take short stays, and what is the shortest stay you will book?

Families in Shelton, Centralia, and Olympia often need a break, not a permanent move.

7. After a hospital stay

Can you take someone leaving the hospital this week, and what care is outside your license?

Discharge desks at the four local hospitals need a yes or no, not a maybe.

8. Hospice arrangements

How does hospice work here, and which agency comes to the house?

The home and the hospice agency are different. Ask who does what at night.

9. Assessment and care plan

Who assesses my person, and when is the written plan updated?

You should be able to see the plan, not only hear that care is personal.

Help with the day

10. Bathing, dressing, grooming

Do you give hands-on help with bathing and dressing, or reminders only?

The difference shows up on the first morning, not in a brochure.

11. Eating

Can you set up meals, prompt a person, and help with feeding if needed?

Ask what happens when appetite drops after a hospital stay.

12. Toileting and continence

How do you help with toileting, and are supplies included in the monthly rate?

Supplies can change the real monthly cost in a private-pay home.

13. Transfers and lifts

Do you use a Hoyer lift or a two-person transfer, and who is trained?

A lift need is one of the first things local discharge planners name.

14. Vision and hearing

How do you help a resident who does not see or hear well?

Ask about lighting, phone volume, and how staff get the person's attention.

Health tasks

15. Medication

Who gives medications, where are they locked, and what if a dose does not match the record?

You want the medication record, not a promise that they are careful.

16. Diabetes

Who checks blood sugar, and can insulin be given here under nurse delegation?

Insulin in a home requires a current nurse-delegation arrangement.

17. Feeding tube

Do you support a feeding tube, and is a nurse delegating that task?

If the answer is unclear, treat it as no until you see the delegation.

18. Wound and ostomy care

Who changes a wound dressing or ostomy pouch, and how is a nurse involved?

This is a licensed task path, not a favor from a caregiver.

19. Oxygen and breathing

Can you support oxygen, and what happens if the power goes out?

Smoke season and winter storms both hit these three counties.

20. Nurse delegation

Which tasks are nurse-delegated here, and when did the nurse last visit?

Ask to see the delegation paperwork, not only a line on a listing.

Who is on duty

21. Nursing

Is a nurse on site, on call, or not part of this home?

An adult family home is not a nursing home. The hours matter.

22. Caregiver coverage

Is a caregiver awake in the house overnight, and who covers a sick day?

Night coverage is the question families forget until the first weekend.

23. Staffing on a shift

How many caregivers are on duty for how many residents on a typical afternoon?

A ratio with no shift attached does not tell you who is in the kitchen at 3 p.m.

24. Visiting clinicians

Which outside people come here: therapy, podiatry, hospice, or primary care?

A trip from Shelton or Chehalis to a clinic is a long day. In-home visits matter.

The house

25. Room choices

Is the open room private or shared, and may we see it today?

The snapshot names a room. You still need to stand in it.

26. Bathrooms

Does this room have its own bathroom, or is the bathroom shared?

Shared bathrooms change both privacy and nighttime help.

27. Getting around the house

Can a wheelchair reach the bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, and an exit?

Ask them to walk the path with you, including the porch and the car.

28. Emergency equipment

Where is the call system, and do you have a working backup for power?

Confirm the generator or battery plan out loud. Do not assume it.

29. Meals

Who cooks, and can you follow a diabetic, low-salt, or texture-modified diet?

Ask what was served yesterday, not what the menu hopes to be.

30. Housekeeping and laundry

Is laundry and room cleaning included, and how often?

If it is extra, add it to the monthly figure before you compare homes.

Three extra questions for this shoreline

Getting to appointments. How do residents get to the doctor in this county: your car, Intercity Transit, Twin Transit, Mason Transit, or a family driver?

Family visits. What are visiting hours, and do adult children coming from Olympia, Centralia, or Shelton need to call ahead?

Smoke and storms. If wildfire smoke or a power outage lasts two days, how do you care for someone who uses oxygen or a lift?

Put the questions on a visit sheet
